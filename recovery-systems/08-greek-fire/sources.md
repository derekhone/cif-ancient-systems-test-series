# Greek Fire — Sources

This document lists the primary and secondary sources cited in the case study.

---

## Primary Sources — Byzantine and Historical Accounts

- Theophanes the Confessor. *Chronographia* (c. 815 CE). Trans. Cyril Mango & Roger Scott (1997). *The Chronicle of Theophanes Confessor: Byzantine and Near Eastern History, AD 284–813*. Oxford: Clarendon Press.
  - Earliest detailed Byzantine chronicle account of Greek Fire's use during the 674–678 Arab siege of Constantinople.

- Constantine VII Porphyrogennetos. *De Administrando Imperio* (c. 950 CE). Trans. R.J.H. Jenkins (1967). Washington, DC: Dumbarton Oaks Research Library.
  - Byzantine emperor's manual on statecraft; includes explicit warnings against revealing the secret of Greek Fire.

- Liutprand of Cremona. *Antapodosis* (c. 960 CE). Trans. F.A. Wright (1930). *The Works of Liutprand of Cremona*. London: Routledge.
  - Western European eyewitness account of Greek Fire's terrifying effects.

- Anna Comnena. *The Alexiad* (c. 1148 CE). Trans. E.R.A. Sewter (1969). London: Penguin.
  - Byzantine princess's history; describes Greek Fire use in 11th-century naval battles.

- Leo VI. *Tactica* (c. 900 CE). Trans. George T. Dennis (2010). *The Taktika of Leo VI*. Washington, DC: Dumbarton Oaks.
  - Byzantine military manual; discusses Greek Fire deployment but provides no formula.

## Secondary Sources — Historical and Technical Analysis

- Partington, J.R. (1960). *A History of Greek Fire and Gunpowder*. Cambridge: W. Heffer & Sons.
  - Standard scholarly treatment; comprehensive history and chemical analysis of proposed reconstructions.

- Haldon, John & Byrne, Maurice (1977). "A Possible Solution to the Problem of Greek Fire." *Byzantinische Zeitschrift* 70: 91–99.
  - Influential modern reconstruction hypothesis: petroleum distillate + quicklime.

- Pryor, John H. & Jeffreys, Elizabeth M. (2006). *The Age of the Dromon: The Byzantine Navy ca. 500–1204*. Leiden: Brill.
  - Comprehensive study of Byzantine naval warfare and Greek Fire's tactical role.

- Roland, Alex (1992). "Secrecy, Technology, and War: Greek Fire and the Defense of Byzantium, 678–1204." *Technology and Culture* 33(4): 655–679.
  - Analysis of Greek Fire as a case study in technological secrecy and its consequences.

---

## Chemical and Reconstruction Analyses

- Forbes, R.J. (1959). *More Studies in Early Petroleum History, 1860–1880*. Leiden: Brill.
  - Historical analysis of petroleum distillates; relevance to Greek Fire hypotheses.

- Needham, Joseph (1986). *Science and Civilisation in China*, vol. 5: *Chemistry and Chemical Technology*, part 7: *Military Technology: The Gunpowder Epic*. Cambridge University Press.
  - Comparative analysis of incendiary weapons; includes discussion of Greek Fire.

- Mayor, Adrienne (2003). *Greek Fire, Poison Arrows, and Scorpion Bombs: Biological and Chemical Warfare in the Ancient World*. New York: Overlook Press.
  - Popular history; includes Greek Fire in broader context of ancient chemical weapons.

---

## Byzantine History and Institutional Context

- Treadgold, Warren (1997). *A History of the Byzantine State and Society*. Stanford University Press.
  - General Byzantine history; context for military and technological development.

- Kazhdan, Alexander P., ed. (1991). *The Oxford Dictionary of Byzantium*. Oxford University Press.
  - Reference work; entry on Greek Fire and related technologies.

- Ahrweiler, Hélène (1966). *Byzance et la mer: La marine de guerre, la politique et les institutions maritimes de Byzance aux VIIe–XVe siècles*. Paris: Presses Universitaires de France.
  - Detailed study of Byzantine naval institutions; context for Greek Fire's development and loss.

---

## Technological Secrecy and Knowledge Loss

- Roland, Alex (2003). "Science, Technology, and War." *Technology and Culture* 44(3): 571–578.
  - Broader discussion of military secrecy and technological knowledge preservation.

- McNeill, William H. (1982). *The Pursuit of Power: Technology, Armed Force, and Society since A.D. 1000*. University of Chicago Press.
  - Historical analysis of military technology and state power; includes Greek Fire as case study.

## CIF and Knowledge Inheritance Context

- Long, Pamela O. (2001). *Openness, Secrecy, Authorship: Technical Arts and the Culture of Knowledge from Antiquity to the Renaissance*. Johns Hopkins University Press.
  - Historical analysis of the tension between secrecy and knowledge transmission in technical fields.

- Mokyr, Joel (2002). *The Gifts of Athena: Historical Origins of the Knowledge Economy*. Princeton University Press.
  - Economic and historical analysis of knowledge creation, transmission, and loss.

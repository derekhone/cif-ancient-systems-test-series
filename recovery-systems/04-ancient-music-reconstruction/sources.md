# Ancient Music Reconstruction — Sources

This document lists the primary and secondary sources cited in the case study.

---

## Primary Sources

- Aristoxenus. *Elementa Harmonica* (4th century BCE). Trans. Henry S. Macran. Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1902.
- Ptolemy, Claudius. *Harmonics* (2nd century CE). Trans. Jon Solomon. Leiden: Brill, 2000.
- Guido d'Arezzo. *Micrologus* (c. 1026). Trans. Warren Babb, in *Hucbald, Guido, and John on Music*. New Haven: Yale University Press, 1978.
- Franco of Cologne. *Ars Cantus Mensurabilis* (c. 1280). Trans. in Strunk's *Source Readings in Music History*, vol. 2.

---

## Secondary Sources — General

- Taruskin, Richard (1995). *Text and Act: Essays on Music and Performance*. Oxford University Press.
  - Key discussion of the distinction between recovering notation versus recovering performance.

- Treitler, Leo (1982). "The Early History of Music Writing in the West." *Journal of the American Musicological Society* 35(2): 237–279.
  - Foundational analysis of early neumes and their limitations as complete performance instructions.

---

## Ancient Greek Music

- West, M.L. (1992). *Ancient Greek Music*. Oxford University Press.
  - Standard scholarly overview of Greek notation, instruments, theory, and surviving fragments.

- Hagel, Stefan (2009). *Ancient Greek Music: A New Technical History*. Cambridge University Press.
  - Detailed technical analysis of Greek tuning systems, notation, and instrumental practice.

- Pöhlmann, Egert & West, M.L. (2001). *Documents of Ancient Greek Music*. Oxford University Press.
  - Critical edition of all surviving Greek musical fragments with transcriptions and commentary.

- Mathiesen, Thomas J. (1999). *Apollo's Lyre: Greek Music and Music Theory in Antiquity and the Middle Ages*. Lincoln: University of Nebraska Press.
  - Comprehensive treatment of Greek music theory and its medieval reception.

---

## Early Medieval Chant

- Levy, Kenneth (1998). *Gregorian Chant and the Carolingians*. Princeton University Press.
  - Authoritative study of the formation and notation of Gregorian chant.

- Hucke, Helmut (1980). "Toward a New Historical View of Gregorian Chant." *Journal of the American Musicological Society* 33(3): 437–467.
  - Critical reassessment of chant origins and the role of notation.

- Kelly, Thomas Forrest (2009). *The Exultet in Southern Italy*. Oxford University Press.
  - Case study of early notation and regional variants.

- Jeffery, Peter (1992). *Re-Envisioning Past Musical Cultures: Ethnomusicology in the Study of Gregorian Chant*. Chicago: University of Chicago Press.
  - Methodological discussion of reconstructing lost performance practice.

---

## Renaissance Polyphony

- Atlas, Allan W. (1998). *Renaissance Music: Music in Western Europe, 1400–1600*. W.W. Norton.
  - Standard textbook with detailed discussion of mensural notation and performance practice.

- Brown, Howard Mayer & Sadie, Stanley, eds. (1989). *Performance Practice: Music Before 1600*. W.W. Norton.
  - Collection of essays on interpreting Renaissance notation.

- Taruskin, Richard (2005). *The Oxford History of Western Music*, vol. 1: *The Earliest Notations to the Sixteenth Century*. Oxford University Press.
  - Comprehensive historical and interpretive overview.

- Bent, Margaret (2002). *Counterpoint, Composition, and Musica Ficta*. Routledge.
  - Technical discussion of what Renaissance notation encoded and what it left implicit.

---

## Performance Practice and Reconstruction

- Butt, John (2002). *Playing with History: The Historical Approach to Musical Performance*. Cambridge University Press.
  - Philosophical and practical discussion of historical performance as reconstruction, not restoration.

- Kenyon, Nicholas, ed. (1988). *Authenticity and Early Music*. Oxford University Press.
  - Collection of essays debating the concept of "authentic" performance and its limits.

- Haynes, Bruce (2007). *The End of Early Music: A Period Performer's History of Music for the Twenty-First Century*. Oxford University Press.
  - Critical perspective on what early music performance can and cannot recover.

---

## Acoustics and Context

- Watson, Ian (2017). *The Historical Harpsichord*, vol. 4: *The Tuning of Musical Instruments*. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press.
  - Technical discussion of historical tuning systems and their reconstruction.

- Blesser, Barry & Salter, Linda-Ruth (2007). *Spaces Speak, Are You Listening? Experiencing Aural Architecture*. MIT Press.
  - Analysis of how architectural acoustics shaped historical music and its modern interpretation.

---

## Notation and Paleography

- Parrish, Carl (1957). *The Notation of Medieval Music*. W.W. Norton.
  - Classic introduction to medieval notation systems.

- Hiley, David (1993). *Western Plainchant: A Handbook*. Oxford University Press.
  - Comprehensive guide to chant notation and manuscript sources.

---

## Methodological Parallels (CIF Context)

- Cook, Nicholas (2013). *Beyond the Score: Music as Performance*. Oxford University Press.
  - Theoretical framework for understanding what notation can and cannot encode.

- Clarke, Eric (2005). *Ways of Listening: An Ecological Approach to the Perception of Musical Meaning*. Oxford University Press.
  - Cognitive and cultural dimensions of musical meaning, relevant to semantic continuity layer analysis.

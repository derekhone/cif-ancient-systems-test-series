# Case Study #7 — Göbekli Tepe · Sources

## Primary Archaeological Sources

**Schmidt, Klaus (2006, 2010, 2011).** "Göbekli Tepe: The Stone Age Sanctuaries — New Results of Ongoing Excavations with a Special Focus on Sculptures and High Reliefs." Multiple publications in *Documenta Praehistorica*, *Neo-Lithics*, and German Archaeological Institute reports.  
*Schmidt was the lead excavator from 1995–2014. His interpretation of the site as a ritual sanctuary for nomadic hunter-gatherers established the foundational scholarly framework.*

**Schmidt, Klaus (2012).** *Göbekli Tepe: A Stone Age Sanctuary in South-Eastern Anatolia.* Ex Oriente, Berlin.  
*Comprehensive monograph on the site's architecture, iconography, and interpretation.*

**Dietrich, Oliver; Heun, Manfred; Notroff, Jens; Schmidt, Klaus; Zarnkow, Martin (2012).** "The role of cult and feasting in the emergence of Neolithic communities. New evidence from Göbekli Tepe, south-eastern Turkey." *Antiquity* 86(333): 674–695.  
DOI: [10.1017/S0003598X00047840](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003598X00047840)  
*Analysis of archaeobotanical and faunal remains; evidence for large-scale feasting and early brewing.*

**Clare, Lee; Dietrich, Oliver; Notroff, Jens; et al. (2019–2021).** Various publications on water management systems, domestic structures, and site formation processes.  
*Recent work challenging Schmidt's "ritual-only" interpretation; evidence for permanent or semi-permanent settlement.*

**Karul, Necmi (2014–present).** Ongoing excavation reports as current project director (Istanbul University / German Archaeological Institute collaboration).  
*Post-Schmidt excavation; focus on expanding understanding of domestic vs. ritual function.*

## Dating and Chronology

**Radiocarbon Dating:** Multiple published dates from organic material in wall plaster and fill deposits confirm primary activity c. 9600–8000 BCE (Pre-Pottery Neolithic A/B).

**Dietrich, Oliver; Köksal-Schmidt, Çiğdem; Notroff, Jens; Schmidt, Klaus (2013).** "Establishing a Radiocarbon Sequence for Göbekli Tepe. State of Research and New Data." *Neo-Lithics* 1/13: 36–41.  
*Methodological discussion of dating challenges and refinements.*

## Architecture and Iconography

**Notroff, Jens; Dietrich, Oliver; Schmidt, Klaus (2014).** "Building Monuments, Creating Communities: Early Monumental Architecture at Pre-Pottery Neolithic Göbekli Tepe." In *Approaching Monumentality in Archaeology*, edited by James F. Osborne. SUNY Press.  
*Analysis of labor organization and communal construction.*

**Dietrich, Oliver & Notroff, Jens (2015).** "A Sanctuary, or So Fair a House? In Defense of an Archaeology of Cult at Pre-Pottery Neolithic Göbekli Tepe." In *Defining the Sacred: Approaches to the Archaeology of Religion in the Near East*, edited by Nicola Laneri. Oxbow Books.  
*Response to critiques of the "ritual center" interpretation.*

**Schmidt, Klaus (2003).** "The 2003 Campaign at Göbekli Tepe (Southeastern Turkey)." *Neo-Lithics* 2/03: 3–8.  
*Early description of T-pillar iconography and anthropomorphic features.*

## T-Pillar Distribution and Regional Context

**Becker, Noémi; Dietrich, Oliver; Götzelt, Thorsten; et al. (2012).** "Materialien zur Deutung der zentralen Pfeilerpaare des Göbekli Tepe und weiterer Orte des obermesopotamischen Frühneolithikums." *Zeitschrift für Orient-Archäologie* 5: 14–43.  
*Survey of T-pillar sites across the region (Nevalı Çori, Karahan Tepe, Harbetsuvan Tepesi); evidence of shared symbolic tradition.*

**Çelik, Bahattin (2011, 2015).** "Karahan Tepe: A New Cultural Centre in the Urfa Area in Turkey." *Documenta Praehistorica* 38: 241–253.  
*Contemporaneous site with similar T-pillar architecture and iconography.*

## Social Organization and Hunter-Gatherer Complexity

**Watkins, Trevor (2010).** "New Light on Neolithic Revolution in South-West Asia." *Antiquity* 84(325): 621–634.  
*Theoretical framework for understanding monumentality and social complexity in pre-agricultural societies.*

**Banning, E. B. (2011).** "So Fair a House: Göbekli Tepe and the Identification of Temples in the Pre-Pottery Neolithic of the Near East." *Current Anthropology* 52(5): 619–660.  
DOI: [10.1086/661207](https://doi.org/10.1086/661207)  
*Critical reassessment of the "temple" designation; argues for multifunctional interpretation.*

**Maher, Lisa A.; Richter, Tobias; Stock, Jay T. (2012).** "The Pre-Natufian Epipaleolithic: Long-term Behavioral Trends in the Levant." *Evolutionary Anthropology* 21(2): 69–81.  
*Broader context of hunter-gatherer complexity in the Near East.*

## Intentional Burial / Backfilling

**Dietrich, Oliver; Notroff, Jens; Schmidt, Klaus (2017).** "Feasting, Social Complexity, and the Emergence of the Early Neolithic of Upper Mesopotamia: A View from Göbekli Tepe." In *Feast, Famine or Fighting?*, edited by Arkadiusz Marciniak et al. McDonald Institute for Archaeological Research.  
*Interpretation of intentional backfilling as ritual decommissioning.*

**Alternative/critical views on burial:** Recent blog posts and conference presentations by Lee Clare and others have questioned whether all backfill was intentional human labor vs. natural slope erosion, earthquakes, or structural collapse. As of 2026, this remains an active area of debate; the mainstream position remains "intentional burial," but natural processes are increasingly acknowledged as contributing factors.

## Subsistence and the Agricultural Transition

**Peters, Joris & Schmidt, Klaus (2004).** "Animals in the symbolic world of Pre-Pottery Neolithic Göbekli Tepe, south-eastern Turkey: a preliminary assessment." *Anthropozoologica* 39(1): 179–218.  
*Faunal analysis: primarily wild game (gazelle, aurochs, wild boar); no evidence of domesticated animals in earliest layers.*

**Dietrich, Laura; Meister, Julia; Dietrich, Oliver; et al. (2019).** "Cereal processing at Early Neolithic Göbekli Tepe, southeastern Turkey." *PLOS ONE* 14(5): e0215214.  
DOI: [10.1371/journal.pone.0215214](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0215214)  
*Evidence of wild cereal processing; grain-grinding tools; early stages of plant domestication.*

**Fuller, Dorian Q.; Willcox, George; Allaby, Robin G. (2011).** "Cultivation and domestication had multiple origins: arguments against the core area hypothesis for the origins of agriculture in the Near East." *World Archaeology* 43(4): 628–652.  
*Broader context of agricultural origins in the Fertile Crescent.*

## Water Management and Settlement Evidence

## Notes on Evidence Quality

- **ESTABLISHED:** Dating, architecture, iconography, and the fact of intentional backfilling are uncontested and documented through peer-reviewed excavation.
- **PLAUSIBLE INFERENCE:** Ritual/communal function, feasting as labor mobilization, and social complexity without hierarchy are mainstream scholarly consensus, though the exact social organization remains debated.
- **SPECULATIVE:** The specific meaning of the iconography, the reasons for burial, and the causal relationship between ritual activity and the agricultural transition are all actively debated and unresolved.

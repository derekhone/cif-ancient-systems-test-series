# Case Study #6 — Polynesian Navigation · Sources

## Primary Works on Polynesian Navigation and Wayfinding

**Lewis, David (1972, 1994).** *We, the Navigators: The Ancient Art of Landfinding in the Pacific.* University of Hawaiʻi Press (2nd edition 1994).  
ISBN: 978-0-8248-1582-9  
*The foundational ethnographic study of Polynesian and Micronesian navigation. Lewis, himself a sailor, documented traditional wayfinding techniques through interviews with master navigators and by sailing with them. Essential primary source.*

**Gladwin, Thomas (1970).** *East Is a Big Bird: Navigation and Logic on Puluwat Atoll.* Harvard University Press.  
ISBN: 978-0-674-22425-0  
*Classic anthropological study of Micronesian navigation in the Caroline Islands. Detailed analysis of the mental models and cognitive structures used by navigators.*

**Kyselka, Will (1987).** *An Ocean in Mind.* University of Hawaiʻi Press.  
ISBN: 978-0-8248-1104-3  
*Accessible account of Polynesian navigation and the early *Hōkūleʻa* voyages. Co-written with input from navigators; emphasis on the star compass and celestial techniques.*

**Finney, Ben (1994).** *Voyage of Rediscovery: A Cultural Odyssey through Polynesia.* University of California Press.  
ISBN: 978-0-520-08278-7  
*Comprehensive history of the Polynesian Voyaging Society and the *Hōkūleʻa* project by one of its founders. Academic rigor combined with first-hand involvement.*

**Finney, Ben (2003).** *Sailing in the Wake of the Ancestors: Reviving Polynesian Voyaging.* Bishop Museum Press.  
ISBN: 978-1-58178-025-0  
*Updated account covering the expansion of the voyaging revival movement across the Pacific.*

**Thompson, Nainoa (various).** Published interviews, articles, and lectures by the first Hawaiian navigator trained in traditional wayfinding after the break in continuity.  
Available through the Polynesian Voyaging Society archives: [https://www.hokulea.com](https://www.hokulea.com)  
*First-hand accounts of learning from Mau Piailug and the restoration of the Hawaiian tradition.*

## Mau Piailug and Micronesian Navigation

**Piailug, Mau (1932–2010).** *Palu* (grandmaster navigator) from Satawal, Micronesia. No published autobiographical works, but extensive interviews and documentary footage exist.

**Low, Sam (2013).** *Hawaiki Rising: Hōkūleʻa, Nainoa Thompson, and the Hawaiian Renaissance.* Island Heritage Publishing.  
ISBN: 978-1-61710-581-4  
*Detailed narrative history focusing on Mau Piailug's role in teaching Nainoa Thompson and the cultural significance of the revival.*

**Feinberg, Richard (1988).** "Polynesian Seafaring and Navigation: Ocean Travel in Anutan Culture and Society." Kent State University Press.  
ISBN: 978-0-87338-361-1  
*Comparative study of navigation traditions across Polynesia; includes discussion of knowledge transmission methods.*

## The Polynesian Voyaging Society and the *Hōkūleʻa*

**Polynesian Voyaging Society (official archives and publications).**  
Website: [https://www.hokulea.com](https://www.hokulea.com)  
*Primary source for voyage logs, navigator interviews, educational materials, and the history of the *Hōkūleʻa* and sister canoes.*

**Finney, Ben; Frost, K. R.; Rhodes, R.; Thompson, Nainoa (1986).** "Wait for the West Wind." *Journal of the Polynesian Society* 95(1): 5–32.  
*Early academic analysis of the 1980 Hawaiʻi–Tahiti round-trip voyage navigated by Nainoa Thompson.*

**Kyselka, Will & Lanterman, Ray E. (1976).** "North Star to Southern Cross." *Oceans* Magazine.  
*Contemporary account of the 1976 inaugural voyage.*

## Archaeological and Historical Context of Pacific Settlement

**Irwin, Geoffrey (1992).** *The Prehistoric Exploration and Colonisation of the Pacific.* Cambridge University Press.  
ISBN: 978-0-521-47651-5  
*Archaeological and simulation-based analysis demonstrating that Polynesian settlement was intentional, not accidental drift.*

**Kirch, Patrick Vinton (2000).** *On the Road of the Winds: An Archaeological History of the Pacific Islands before European Contact.* University of California Press.  
ISBN: 978-0-520-22347-1  
*Comprehensive synthesis of Pacific prehistory; settlement chronology and cultural continuities.*

**Anderson, Atholl; Chappell, John; Gagan, Michael; Grove, Richard (2006).** "Prehistoric Maritime Migration in the Pacific Islands: An Hypothesis of ENSO Forcing." *The Holocene* 16(1): 5–17.  
DOI: [10.1191/0959683606hl891pp](https://doi.org/10.1191/0959683606hl891pp)  
*Climate and oceanographic analysis relevant to understanding conditions for ancient voyaging.*

**Wilmshurst, Janet M.; Hunt, Terry L.; Lipo, Carl P.; Anderson, Atholl J. (2011).** "High-precision radiocarbon dating shows recent and rapid initial human colonization of East Polynesia." *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 108(5): 1815–1820.  
DOI: [10.1073/pnas.1015876108](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1015876108)  
*Refined chronology of Polynesian expansion; confirms late but rapid settlement of remote islands like Hawaiʻi, Easter Island, and New Zealand.*

## Navigation Techniques and Cognitive Science

**Hutchins, Edwin (1995).** *Cognition in the Wild.* MIT Press.  
ISBN: 978-0-262-58146-2  
*Influential work on distributed cognition, with case studies including modern naval navigation. Relevant for understanding how navigational knowledge is distributed across people, tools, and environment.*

**D'Andrade, Roy (1995).** *The Development of Cognitive Anthropology.* Cambridge University Press.  
ISBN: 978-0-521-45976-1  
*Theoretical framework for understanding cultural knowledge systems; includes discussion of navigation as a cognitive domain.*

**Genz, Joseph H. (2011).** "Navigating the Revival of Voyaging in the Marshall Islands: Predicaments of Preservation and Possibilities of Collaboration." *The Contemporary Pacific* 23(1): 1–34.  
DOI: [10.1353/cp.2011.0004](https://doi.org/10.1353/cp.2011.0004)  
*Case study of navigation revival in another Pacific region; lessons about knowledge transmission and cultural revitalization.*

## Notes on Evidence Quality

- **ESTABLISHED:** The 1976 *Hōkūleʻa* voyage and subsequent successful long-distance non-instrument navigation are documented and reproducible. The core techniques (star compass, swell piloting, bird navigation) are observable and have been validated scientifically.
- **PLAUSIBLE INFERENCE:** The claim that Hawaiian wayfinding could not have been revived without Mau Piailug is strongly supported by the historical record—no successful revival occurred before his teaching, and all subsequent Hawaiian navigators trace their lineage to Mau's instruction.
- **SPECULATIVE:** The precise nature of pre-contact Hawaiian navigation (before Western contact in 1778) and how much was lost even in the Micronesian traditions that survived are matters of ongoing research and debate.

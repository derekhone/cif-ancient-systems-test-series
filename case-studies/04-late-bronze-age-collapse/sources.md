# Case Study #4 — Late Bronze Age Collapse · Sources

## Primary Scholarly Works

**Cline, Eric H. (2014).** *1177 B.C.: The Year Civilization Collapsed.* Princeton University Press.  
ISBN: 978-0-691-16788-6  
*The foundational modern synthesis of the "perfect storm" / systems collapse model. Cline argues for multicausal collapse driven by simultaneous drought, earthquakes, trade disruption, and the Sea Peoples.*

**Cline, Eric H. (2021).** *1177 B.C.: The Year Civilization Collapsed (Revised and Updated Edition).* Princeton University Press.  
ISBN: 978-0-691-20801-5  
*Updated edition incorporating recent archaeological findings and climate data.*

**Drews, Robert (1993).** *The End of the Bronze Age: Changes in Warfare and the Catastrophe ca. 1200 B.C.* Princeton University Press.  
ISBN: 978-0-691-04811-6  
*Influential but contested argument that changes in military technology and tactics (specifically the rise of infantry warfare) triggered the collapse. Now largely superseded by multicausal models.*

**Yasur-Landau, Assaf (2010).** *The Philistines and Aegean Migration at the End of the Late Bronze Age.* Cambridge University Press.  
DOI: [10.1017/CBO9780511760051](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511760051)  
*Archaeological analysis of migration patterns and the "Sea Peoples" in the Levant; DNA and material culture evidence.*

**Middleton, Guy D. (2010).** "The Collapse of Palatial Society in LBA Greece and the Postpalatial Period." PhD dissertation, Durham University.  
*Detailed examination of the Mycenaean collapse; challenges older "catastrophe" models, argues for more gradual and regionally variable processes.*

**Middleton, Guy D. (2017).** *Understanding Collapse: Ancient History and Modern Myths.* Cambridge University Press.  
DOI: [10.1017/9781316584941](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781316584941)  
*Theoretical framework for understanding societal collapse; critique of overly simplistic monocausal explanations.*

## The Amarna Letters and Diplomatic Context

**Moran, William L. (1992).** *The Amarna Letters.* Johns Hopkins University Press.  
ISBN: 978-0-8018-4251-1  
*English translation of the Amarna diplomatic correspondence (14th century BCE); primary source evidence for the "Club of Great Powers" and interconnected Late Bronze Age world.*

**Liverani, Mario (2001).** *International Relations in the Ancient Near East, 1600–1100 BC.* Palgrave Macmillan.  
ISBN: 978-0-333-76093-3  
*Analysis of diplomatic protocols, trade networks, and power dynamics in the Late Bronze Age.*

## The Sea Peoples

**Cline, Eric H. & O'Connor, David (eds., 2012).** *Ramesses III: The Life and Times of Egypt's Last Hero.* University of Michigan Press.  
ISBN: 978-0-472-11877-5  
*Analysis of Ramesses III's reign, the battles against the Sea Peoples, and the Medinet Habu reliefs.*

**Sandars, N. K. (1985).** *The Sea Peoples: Warriors of the Ancient Mediterranean, 1250–1150 BC.* Thames & Hudson (revised edition).  
ISBN: 978-0-500-27387-6  
*Classic overview; somewhat dated but still useful for synthesizing Egyptian sources.*

**Killebrew, Ann E. & Lehmann, Gunnar (eds., 2013).** *The Philistines and Other "Sea Peoples" in Text and Archaeology.* Society of Biblical Literature.  
ISBN: 978-1-58983-721-8  
*Archaeological and textual evidence for Sea Peoples identity, origins, and settlement patterns.*

## Environmental and Climate Evidence

**Kaniewski, David; Van Campo, Elise; Guiot, Joel; et al. (2013).** "Environmental Roots of the Late Bronze Age Crisis." *PLOS ONE* 8(8): e71004.  
DOI: [10.1371/journal.pone.0071004](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0071004)  
*Pollen analysis demonstrating prolonged drought and agricultural collapse in the Eastern Mediterranean c. 1250–1100 BCE.*

**Finné, Martin; Holmgren, Karin; Sundqvist, Hanna S.; et al. (2011).** "Climate in the Eastern Mediterranean, and Adjacent Regions, during the Past 6000 Years – A Review." *Journal of Archaeological Science* 38(12): 3153–3173.  
DOI: [10.1016/j.jas.2011.05.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jas.2011.05.007)  
*Synthesis of paleoclimatic data; evidence for "megadrought" during Late Bronze Age collapse period.*

**Drake, Brandon L. (2012).** "The Influence of Climatic Change on the Late Bronze Age Collapse and the Greek Dark Ages." *Journal of Archaeological Science* 39(6): 1862–1870.  
DOI: [10.1016/j.jas.2012.01.029](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jas.2012.01.029)  
*Quantitative analysis linking drought severity to societal collapse.*

**Langgut, Dafna; Finkelstein, Israel; Litt, Thomas (2013).** "Climate and the Late Bronze Collapse: New Evidence from the Southern Levant." *Tel Aviv* 40(2): 149–175.  
DOI: [10.1179/033443513X13753505864205](https://doi.org/10.1179/033443513X13753505864205)  
*Pollen data from the Sea of Galilee region; drought and agricultural stress.*

## Earthquake Storms

**Nur, Amos & Cline, Eric H. (2000).** "Poseidon's Horses: Plate Tectonics and Earthquake Storms in the Late Bronze Age Aegean and Eastern Mediterranean." *Journal of Archaeological Science* 27(1): 43–63.  
DOI: [10.1006/jasc.1999.0431](https://doi.org/10.1006/jasc.1999.0431)  
*Geological and archaeological evidence for a series of major earthquakes coinciding with the collapse.*

**Nur, Amos (2008).** *Apocalypse: Earthquakes, Archaeology, and the Wrath of God.* Princeton University Press.  
ISBN: 978-0-691-13975-3  
*Popular synthesis arguing for earthquakes as a major contributor to Bronze Age destructions.*

## Palace Economies and Linear B

**Ventris, Michael & Chadwick, John (1956, 1973).** *Documents in Mycenaean Greek.* Cambridge University Press (2nd edition 1973).  
ISBN: 978-0-521-08558-8  
*The foundational decipherment of Linear B; includes transliterations and translations of the major archives.*

**Palaima, Thomas G. (2011).** "Scribes, Scribal Hands and Palaeography." In *A Companion to Linear B: Mycenaean Greek Texts and their World*, edited by Yves Duhoux & Anna Morpurgo Davies. Peeters.  
*Analysis of Linear B as a specialized administrative tool; scribal training and palace bureaucracy.*

**Bennet, John (2007).** "Pylos: The Expansion of a Mycenaean Palatial Centre." In *Rethinking Mycenaean Palaces II*, edited by Michael L. Galaty & William A. Parkinson. Cotsen Institute of Archaeology.  
*Detailed study of the Pylos palace economy and its administrative records.*

**Shelmerdine, Cynthia W. (2008).** "Background, Sources, and Methods." In *The Cambridge Companion to the Aegean Bronze Age*, edited by Cynthia W. Shelmerdine. Cambridge University Press.  
*Overview of Linear B as evidence for Mycenaean economy and society.*

## The Greek Dark Ages and Literacy Loss

**Whitley, James (1991, 2001).** *The Archaeology of Ancient Greece.* Cambridge University Press.  
ISBN: 978-0-521-62733-1  
*Survey of the transition from Bronze Age to Iron Age; the "Dark Age" as a period of transformation rather than total collapse.*

**Osborne, Robin (1996).** *Greece in the Making, 1200–479 BC.* Routledge.  
ISBN: 978-0-415-03583-9  
*Synthesis of early Greek history; the loss and reintroduction of writing.*

**Powell, Barry B. (1991).** *Homer and the Origin of the Greek Alphabet.* Cambridge University Press.  
ISBN: 978-0-521-58907-9  
*Analysis of the adoption of the Phoenician alphabet around 800 BCE; argues it was adapted specifically to record Homeric epic.*

**Petropoulos, John C. B. (2011).** "The Dark Age and Its Legacy." In *A Companion to the Archaeology of the Ancient Near East*, edited by D. T. Potts. Wiley-Blackwell.  
*Cultural continuity and memory across the illiterate period.*

## Systems Collapse Theory

**Tainter, Joseph A. (1988).** *The Collapse of Complex Societies.* Cambridge University Press.  
ISBN: 978-0-521-38673-9  
*Classic theoretical framework for understanding societal collapse; diminishing returns on complexity.*

**Scheffer, Marten; Carpenter, Steve; Foley, Jonathan A.; Folke, Carl; Walker, Brian (2001).** "Catastrophic Shifts in Ecosystems." *Nature* 413: 591–596.  
DOI: [10.1038/35098000](https://doi.org/10.1038/35098000)  
*Ecological systems theory; tipping points and cascading failures (applicable to social systems).*

**Homer-Dixon, Thomas (2006).** *The Upside of Down: Catastrophe, Creativity, and the Renewal of Civilization.* Island Press.  
ISBN: 978-1-59726-065-4  
*Analysis of systemic fragility in complex, interconnected systems; relevance to modern global networks.*

ISBN: 978-0-415-35935-9  
*Detailed regional synthesis; challenges uniform "collapse" narrative.*

**Iacovou, Maria (2013).** "Historically Elusive and Internally Fragile Island Polities: The Intricacies of Cyprus's Political Geography in the Iron Age." *Bulletin of the American Schools of Oriental Research* 370: 15–47.  
DOI: [10.5615/bullamerschoorie.370.0015](https://doi.org/10.5615/bullamerschoorie.370.0015)

ISBN: 978-0-500-29196-2

## Notes on Evidence Quality

- **ESTABLISHED:** The timeline and geography of destruction/abandonment, the existence of palace economies, the loss of Linear B, the Amarna diplomatic network, and the Egyptian records of the Sea Peoples are all uncontested.
- **PLAUSIBLE INFERENCE:** The "perfect storm" / systems collapse model is mainstream scholarly consensus, supported by converging archaeological, paleoclimatic, and textual evidence.
- **SPECULATIVE:** The precise causal sequence (which stressor came first, which was most important) and the specific motivations/origins of the Sea Peoples remain debated.

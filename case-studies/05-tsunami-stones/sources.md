# Sources — Case Study #5: Japanese Tsunami Stones

## Contemporaneous primary reporting

- Fackler, M. (2011, April 20). "Tsunami Warnings, Written in Stone." *The New York Times*.
  - The foundational English-language report on Aneyoshi and the tsunami-stone tradition following the 2011 Tōhoku disaster. Source for the Aneyoshi inscription and the account that 2011 floodwaters stopped below the marker.

## Documentation and analysis

- *Smithsonian Magazine* (2011), "Century-Old Warnings Against Tsunamis Dot Japan's Coastline." — Prof. Fumihiko Imamura (Tohoku University) on the ~three-generation memory-decay pattern; overview of mixed compliance as coastlines modernized.
- Bressan, D. (2021, March 11). "Century-Old Tsunami Stones Saved Lives In The Tohoku Earthquake Of 2011." *Forbes*. — Review of the stones' function and the 1896/1933/2011 sequence.
- 99% Invisible, "Tsunami Stones: Ancient Japanese Markers Warn Builders of High Water." — On the 600+ stones, inscription types (instruction vs. memorial vs. mass-grave marker), and the Aneyoshi relocation history.
- *Wikipedia*, "Tsunami stone." — Aggregated overview with onward citations; used for cross-checking dates and counts, not as a primary source.
- Hunchuck, E. M. "An Incomplete Atlas of Stones." *The Funambulist* (Cartography & Power). — Cross-disciplinary documentation project mapping stone coordinates and elevations relative to historical inundation lines, including the 2011 Great East Japan Earthquake tsunami; source for the "shared legibility" framing and methodology.

## Disaster / geological context

- 1896 Meiji-Sanriku earthquake and tsunami: ~22,000 deaths (widely reported figure).
- 1933 Shōwa-Sanriku earthquake and tsunami: motivated the 1933 Aneyoshi stone; village reduced to four survivors (as reported).
- 2011 Tōhoku (Great East Japan) earthquake and tsunami: on the order of ~16,000–18,000 deaths; the single event that simultaneously stress-tested every stone on the coast.
- Historical tsunami and storm deposits, Sanriku coast (peer-reviewed deposit studies, e.g., work published in *Sedimentary Geology* / *Marine Geology*) — independent geological confirmation of the recurrent tsunami hazard the stones encode.

## Modern coastal-management context

- Post-2011 seawall construction and community negotiation (e.g., Kesennuma) — documented tension between engineered defenses and local/traditional risk knowledge (recent DRR literature, ScienceDirect S2212420925006946). Used to support the "new capability displaces old control" analysis.

## Notes on evidence handling

- The existence, inscriptions, and Aneyoshi survival are treated as ESTABLISHED. The *causal* link between the stone specifically and the survival outcome is treated as PLAUSIBLE INFERENCE, because settlement elevation was reinforced by the 1933 relocation, schooling, and living memory — the stone is one node in a system, not a sole cause. The mixed compliance record (many stones ignored) is treated as data of equal weight to the Aneyoshi success, to avoid a confirmation-only reading.
- Where death tolls are given as ranges, this reflects genuine variation across reported figures; no single precise counterfactual toll is asserted.

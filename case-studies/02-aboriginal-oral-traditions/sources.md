# Sources — Case Study #2: Aboriginal Australian Oral Traditions

## Primary peer-reviewed source

- Nunn, P. D., & Reid, N. J. (2016). "Aboriginal Memories of Inundation of the Australian Coast Dating from More than 7000 Years Ago." *Australian Geographer*, 47(1), 11–47. DOI: 10.1080/00049182.2015.1077539.
  - The foundational study. 21 groups of Aboriginal stories cross-referenced against post-glacial sea-level reconstructions. Reports specific location datings including Port Phillip Bay (~7,800–9,350 yr), Kangaroo Island (~9,800–10,650 yr), Spencer Gulf (~9,000–12,450 yr), and the Great Barrier Reef / Fitzroy Island region (up to ~12,600 yr).

## Skeptical / critical scholarship (mainstream caution)

- Henige, D. "Impossible to Disprove Yet Impossible to Believe: The Unforgiving Epistemology of Deep-Time Oral Tradition." (ResearchGate record; critique of the falsifiability of deep-time oral tradition claims.)
  - Argues the deep-time-memory inference can be neither verified nor falsified and presumes an idealized set of transmission constraints.
- Hiscock, P. — cited in coverage and in the deep-time oral tradition debate; argues the dating risks circularity (assuming a story is faithful geological observation in order to date it).
- "A history of deep time: Indigenous knowledges and deep pasts in settler-colonial presents." (2021, ResearchGate 356475800) — situates the debate in its scholarly and political context.
- SAGE Journals (2023), "17506980231203645" — memory-studies treatment of the transmission-as-replication assumption.

## Supporting mechanism / context

- Reid, N. — commentary on "cross-generational cross-checking" and kin-based verification (as reported in *The Guardian*, 16 Sep 2015).
- *Scientific American*, "Ancient Sea Rise Tale Told Accurately for 10,000 Years" — accessible summary of the mechanism (three-generation simultaneous custodianship acting as error-correction).
- University of the Sunshine Coast news (2016): the paper became the most-read article in the 116-year history of *Australian Geographer*.
- PACJA (2023): "Songlines, memory and relational knowledge systems" — on landscape-anchored mnemonics and multimodal encoding.
- University of Tasmania (2023): Tasmanian Aboriginal oral traditions among the oldest recorded narratives.

## Independent geological anchor (sea-level rise)

- Post-glacial sea-level rise of ~120–130 m from the Last Glacial Maximum to ~7,000 years ago is established from multiple independent glaciological and geological datasets (standard Quaternary sea-level literature). Used here as the external check against which the oral accounts are compared.

## Notes on evidence handling

- The geological record and the existence/content of the traditions are treated as ESTABLISHED. The linkage inference (that the stories are accurate deep-time memories of specific events) is treated as CONTESTED/SPECULATIVE and presented alongside the Henige/Hiscock critique. No datings are asserted as certain; ranges are reproduced as reported by Nunn & Reid.

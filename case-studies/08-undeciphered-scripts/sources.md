# Case Study #8 — Undeciphered Scripts · Sources

## Indus Valley Script

### Primary Scholarly Sources

**Parpola, Asko (1994, 2009).** *Deciphering the Indus Script*. Cambridge University Press.  
*Comprehensive treatment of the Dravidian hypothesis and structural analysis of the script. Parpola is one of the leading scholars in Indus studies.*

**Mahadevan, Iravatham (1977, 2002).** *The Indus Script: Texts, Concordance and Tables*. Archaeological Survey of India.  
*Authoritative catalog and structural analysis of Indus inscriptions.*

**Possehl, Gregory L. (1996, 2002).** *Indus Age: The Writing System*. University of Pennsylvania Press.  
*Archaeological context and sign inventory analysis.*

**Kenoyer, Jonathan Mark (1998, 2008).** *Ancient Cities of the Indus Valley Civilization*. Oxford University Press / American Institute of Pakistan Studies.  
*Archaeological context of seals, trade networks, and urban structure.*

### The "Non-Linguistic" Debate

**Farmer, Steve; Sproat, Richard; Witzel, Michael (2004).** "The Collapse of the Indus-Script Thesis: The Myth of a Literate Harappan Civilization." *Electronic Journal of Vedic Studies* 11(2): 19–57.  
*Controversial paper arguing the Indus symbols are non-linguistic emblems, not phonetic writing. Widely contested but raises serious methodological questions about corpus size and text length.*

**Rao, Rajesh P. N.; Yadav, Nisha; Vahia, Mayank N.; Joglekar, Hrishikesh; Adhikari, R.; Mahadevan, Iravatham (2009).** "Entropic Evidence for Linguistic Structure in the Indus Script." *Science* 324(5931): 1165.  
DOI: [10.1126/science.1170391](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1170391)  
*Computational analysis arguing that sign distribution patterns are consistent with linguistic systems, not random emblems. Response to Farmer et al.*

**Sproat, Richard (2010).** "Ancient Symbols, Computational Linguistics, and the Reviewing Practices of the General Science Journals." *Computational Linguistics* 36(3): 585–594.  
*Counter-response arguing that statistical regularities do not prove linguistic encoding.*

### Archaeological Context

**Marshall, John (1931).** *Mohenjo-daro and the Indus Civilization* (3 vols). Arthur Probsthain, London.  
*Foundational excavation reports.*

**Vidale, Massimo (2023).** "Seal function, administrative practice and élite identity at Harappa." *Nature: Humanities and Social Sciences Communications* 10, Article 863.  
DOI: [10.1038/s41599-023-02320-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41599-023-02320-7)  
*Recent analysis of seal use in administrative and commercial contexts.*

**Wright, Rita P. (2010).** *The Ancient Indus: Urbanism, Economy, and Society*. Cambridge University Press.  
*Social and economic context of the Indus civilization.*

### Corpus Size and Decipherment Theory

**Robinson, Andrew (2002, 2009).** *Lost Languages: The Enigma of the World's Undeciphered Scripts*. McGraw-Hill / Thames & Hudson.  
*Comparative treatment of decipherment challenges across multiple scripts.*

**Barber, Elizabeth Wayland (1974).** *Archaeological Decipherment: A Handbook*. Princeton University Press.  
*Methodological framework for assessing decipherability; includes corpus-size thresholds for unknown languages.*

*Comprehensive catalog and controversial decipherment attempt (the "creation chant" reading). Fischer's linguistic claims are contested.*

**Barthel, Thomas S. (1958).** *Grundlagen zur Entzifferung der Osterinselschrift*. Cram, de Gruyter & Co., Hamburg.

### Recent Radiocarbon Dating and Context

**Ferrara, Silvia; Montecchi, Barbara; Valério, Miguel; Elkins, Nathan (2024).** "Easter Island's Rongorongo script predates European contact." *Nature: Scientific Reports* 14, Article 2579.

## CIF-Relevant Interpretations

**Why these scripts remain undeciphered (synthesized):**

1. **No bilingual anchor.** No "Rosetta Stone" equivalent exists for any of the three scripts.
2. **Unknown or extinct languages.** Indus: language unknown, possibly Dravidian, Munda, Indo-Aryan, or extinct isolate. Linear A: Minoan language is an isolate with no known relatives (not Greek, not IE, not Semitic). Rongorongo: the Rapa Nui oral key was destroyed; uncertain whether glyphs are full phonetic encoding or mnemonic prompts.
3. **Corpus size and text brevity.** Indus: average 5 signs/text, longest ~30. Linear A: ~1,400 texts, mostly formulaic admin records. Rongorongo: ~27 tablets, repetitive structure. All fall below optimal corpus thresholds for linguistic reconstruction of unknown languages.
4. **Catastrophic cultural discontinuity.** Harappan urban collapse ~1900 BCE; Minoan palace destructions ~1450 BCE; Rapa Nui population collapse 1860s (targeted killing of literate elite). In all cases, the cultural receiver that made the script readable was annihilated or disrupted, with no redundancy or fallback.

**Modern computational approaches:**
- Machine learning, statistical pattern analysis, and entropy calculations have identified structural regularities (Indus, Linear A) consistent with linguistic systems.
- These methods refine understanding of data structure (Pillar 2) but have not yet recovered semantic meaning (Pillar 5).
- AI-driven approaches remain active research areas but have not produced consensus decipherments.

## Notes on Evidence Quality

- **ESTABLISHED:** The physical artifacts, their archaeological contexts, the sign inventories, and the corpus catalogs are uncontested and peer-reviewed.
- **PLAUSIBLE INFERENCE:** The linguistic hypotheses (e.g., Dravidian for Indus) and the structural arguments for why decipherment has failed (corpus size, no bilingual, extinct language) are mainstream scholarly consensus, though debated in specifics.
- **SPECULATIVE:** Specific decipherment claims (of which there have been over 100 for Indus alone) are almost universally rejected by the scholarly community. The "non-linguistic" hypothesis for Indus is a serious minority position but not consensus.
